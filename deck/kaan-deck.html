A voice-first fact-check for forwarded WhatsApp voice notes in India. Forward a suspicious note; hear what is true in 20 seconds, in the same language, with the source named aloud.

Working prototype · runs with docker compose up · no API keys required

1 · Problem

In India, misinformation arrives as audio — and every correction is text.

A forwarded WhatsApp voice note is the default format for anyone who does not type comfortably: a 40-second warning, a rumour, a fake remedy, in Hindi, Bhojpuri or Tamil.

Every fact-checker in the country works in text and English. By the time an article is published — 24 to 72 hours later — the note has already reached five family groups.

And the person who most needs the correction cannot read it.

This is a format problem, not a truth problem. The correction fails because it is the wrong shape.

A text link dies at the edge of a voice-note group. It has to be played, not read — and it has to be forwardable back into the same thread.

2 · Solution

Same shape as the misinformation: audio in, audio out.

01
Forward the note
Speak it, upload the file, or paste the text.
02
One claim
A 40-second ramble is reduced to the single checkable assertion.
03
Verified source
Matched against PIB Fact Check, WHO, Ministry of Health.
04
20-second verdict
Spoken in Hindi or English, as chosen. The source is named aloud.
05
Forward it back
A clean audio clip that can return to the group it came from.

What the user hears (example):

“आपके नोट में कहा गया है कि गर्म पानी में नींबू डालकर पीने से कोरोना खत्म हो जाता है। WHO के COVID-19 mythbusters के मुताबिक यह गलत है। … Source: WHO, 2020।”

And when it cannot verify:

“हम इसे verify नहीं कर पाए। … बिना सोर्स के हम सच या झूठ नहीं कहेंगे।” No verdict without a named source — enforced in code, not in a prompt.

3 · Who it is for

Two people, and they are different people.

The listener

100M+

Our estimate of adults in tier-2 and tier-3 India who receive forwarded voice notes but rarely read English text. They hear a claim, believe it within a minute, and forward it on.

The family fact-checker

10s of M

Usually the 25-year-old who gets forwarded “is this true?” several times a week, spends twenty minutes searching, and is ignored — a link is not an answer.

The group

150–250

People in one family or village group. A single verdict reaches all of them, because audio is the one format the group actually plays.

Beachhead: one language (Hindi), one vertical (health misinformation), one district. Frequency: several notes a week, spiking during elections, outbreaks and exam-result season.

4 · What we built

A working prototype of one core flow — not a mockup.

  • 3 bundled Hindi voice notes with real neural audio, plus 10 curated verified claims with named sources and dates.
  • Three ways in: speak it, upload the audio file (for anyone who cannot speak), or paste the text.
  • Claim extraction + matching written from scratch: weighted token signatures, split by script, 1–5 ms per check.
  • Refusal path with its own spoken answer.
  • Audio verdict out in Hindi and English — the toggle switches the spoken answer too, not just the labels.
  • Forward actions: download the clip, or share via WhatsApp.

Runs anywhere

docker compose up → port 3000. No .env, no secret keys, no network calls at runtime. ~3,950 lines of our own code.

Verified in the container

Home page, CSS, audio files and the check API all return 200 inside the built image; the local matcher answers in single-digit milliseconds.

5 · What it depends on

Every dependency has a fallback. None of them can take the product down.

DependencyUsed forIf it gets costly or stops working
Verified sources
PIB Fact Check, WHO, MoHFW		 The verdict itself The corpus is curated and versioned in the repo, not scraped live. If a source goes offline, affected claims are pulled and the note returns unverified — the refusal path is the fallback.
Bundled TTS audio
pre-generated, committed		 Verdict voice, both languages No runtime dependency at all for known claims — Hindi and English tracks both ship in the image. New text falls back to the browser's own voice, then to on-screen text.
Speech → text
Web Speech API · Whisper if keyed		 Live microphone and audio upload Optional. The bundled notes ship with transcripts and are recognised by fingerprint with no key at all; with no key, unknown uploads say so and ask for text instead of guessing.
Optional LLM
OpenAI, if a key exists		 Claim extraction Off by default. The local rule-based extractor is the default path; a failed or rate-limited call silently falls back to it.
WhatsApp
production delivery		 Receiving notes and replying Business API cost and policy risk are real. The logic is channel-agnostic: SMS and IVR can carry the same audio verdict for feature phones.

Cost shape: the demo path has zero marginal cost. At scale, speech-to-text and text-to-speech dominate — so verdicts are cached, trending claims are pre-computed, and the refusal path avoids paid calls entirely.

6 · Launch plan

Start where the forwards already travel.

Weeks 0–6 · one district

One language, one vertical: health misinformation in Hindi. Partner with two regional newsrooms and one NGO network. Recruit 50 community verifiers — teachers, ASHA workers, college students — to put the number into groups they are already in.

Months 2–3 · prove the loop

Track re-forward rate (target 30%), time-to-correction (under five minutes) and seven-day repeat use (target 40%). Publish the refusal rate. Add Marathi and Tamil.

Months 6–12 · scale

WhatsApp Business API, newsroom syndication, IVR for feature phones. Revenue from newsroom licensing and CSR partnerships — not from the people who need the correction.

Every verdict is an audio ad inside the exact group it corrects. The product distributes itself in the format it defends.

7 · Risk

The main risk is being confidently wrong.

A fact-checker that errs once is worse than none: it launders a false claim through institutional authority, and audio makes that wrong verdict maximally shareable.

A small corpus also means high refusal rates on novel local claims — and a fact-checker that says “I don't know” too often loses the group.

  • No verdict without a named source, spoken aloud in the audio.
  • Refusal is a first-class answer, with its own voice and screen.
  • Human review before any new claim enters the corpus.
  • No politics, no opinions, no medical advice beyond quoting the source.
  • Refusal rate published, so the guardrail is measured, not assumed.
